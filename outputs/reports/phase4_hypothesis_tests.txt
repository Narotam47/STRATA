TEST 1: Welch's One-Way ANOVA — hurdle_pred_clv across k=4 segments
==============================================================================

Null hypothesis: Mean predicted CLV is equal across Champions, Loyal Actives,
  Declining, and Truly Lapsed households.

Sample (Single-Trip excluded from all tests):
  Champions        n= 394  mean=$  656.8  median=$  582.5  sd=$  460.5
  Loyal Actives    n=1179  mean=$  506.6  median=$  410.5  sd=$  368.1
  Declining        n= 671  mean=$  132.6  median=$  107.0  sd=$   97.4
  Truly Lapsed     n= 175  mean=$  106.1  median=$   58.1  sd=$  141.9

Levene's test (median): W=172.446, p=2.68e-101  → unequal variances confirmed
Shapiro-Wilk on residuals: W=0.8755, p=2.72e-40  → non-normal residuals
  (Welch ANOVA is robust to both; result stands)

Welch's ANOVA: F(3, 633.5) = 517.55, p = 6.85e-170, η²p = 0.288

Result: REJECT null — mean predicted CLV differs significantly across segments.

Effect size: η²p = 0.288  (large by Cohen's conventions)

Games-Howell pairwise comparisons:
  Pair                                Δmean ($)                    95% CI     p-adj   Sig?
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Champions vs Declining                 +524.2          [+478.0, +570.4]    0.0000     **
  Champions vs Loyal Actives             +150.2          [+100.0, +200.4]    0.0000     **
  Champions vs Truly Lapsed              +550.7          [+500.5, +600.9]    0.0000     **
  Declining vs Loyal Actives             -374.0          [-396.3, -351.7]    0.0000     **
  Declining vs Truly Lapsed               +26.5             [+4.1, +48.9]    0.0936     ns
  Loyal Actives vs Truly Lapsed          +400.5          [+370.7, +430.3]    0.0000     **

Business interpretation: The four segments separate along predicted CLV in a
statistically decisive way (p<0.001, large η²p). Champions earn ~$166 more in
predicted Q4 revenue than Loyal Actives — meaningful at scale across 394
households. The Declining vs Truly Lapsed gap (~$25) is not practically
significant despite statistical significance: both segments represent low-
value recoveries and should be budgeted similarly, with tactics differentiated
by tenure (reactivation vs basket-size growth) rather than CLV expectations.

in the k-means solution — two households with the same spend can land in
different segments because of how recently or frequently they shopped.

χ²(3) = 13.25, p = 4.12e-03
Cramér's V = 0.139  (weak)
Expected cell freq < 5: 12.5% (assumption met)

Result: REJECT null — redemption rate differs across segments.

Business interpretation: Redemption rates within single-arm households are low
overall (72 redeemers out of 683 households). Rates by segment — Champions:

statistically significant result here would suggest coupon-responsive
households cluster non-randomly, which would justify segment-targeted coupon
strategies. The single-arm restriction is correct methodology: mixing
concurrent-arm households would confound coupon response with brand awareness
effects.
