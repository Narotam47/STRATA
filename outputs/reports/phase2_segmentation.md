## 2. Why k = 4, Not k = 2

The metric optimum at k = 2 produces a trivially interpretable
split: one cluster of high-frequency households and one of low-frequency
households. A marketing team cannot act on two groups with no further
differentiation — there is no distinct intervention type for "active" vs
"inactive" at that granularity.

**k = 4 is a deliberate business decision**, not a metric choice. It is the
smallest k at which the panel yields four segments with qualitatively
distinct behavioural profiles, each mapping to a different campaign type:

| Segment | Primary lever |
|---|---|
| Champions | Retention; loyalty rewards to prevent defection |
| Loyal Actives | Upsell basket size; cross-category expansion |
| Declining | Win-back; identify the widening gap before full lapse |
| Truly Lapsed | Reactivation or write-off; CLV too low for spend-heavy outreach |

The automated decision rule (silhouette ≥ 0.90 × peak) recommended k = 2 on
the most recent run. That recommendation is overridden by the module-level
constant `CHOSEN_K = 4` and logged to stdout at runtime.

not an artefact of a particular random initialisation or data draw.

## 5. Central Finding — The Declining / Truly Lapsed Split

RFM scoring collapses both Declining and Truly Lapsed households into a
single "Lost" or "At-Risk" bucket because both groups have low recency and
low frequency scores. K-means with behavioural features separates them into
two actionable groups:

**Declining** (n ≈ 671): median tenure
316 days,
median recency 26 days.
Long-tenured households whose inter-visit gap has been widening in the last
60–90 days. They were loyal and are drifting — the profile of a household
responding to a competitor promotion or a life-event change. A targeted
win-back offer sent before the gap widens past 45 days is likely cost-effective.

**Truly Lapsed** (n ≈ 191): median tenure
150 days,
median recency 89 days.
Short-tenured households that tried the store and stopped — frequently new
or opportunistic shoppers with no established cadence. Their predicted Q4 CLV
overlaps with Declining (bootstrap CIs overlap: Declining [$125, $140],
Truly Lapsed [$87, $131]), meaning they cannot be ranked against each other
by CLV alone. The discriminating feature is **tenure**: Declining households
have ≥ 2× the median tenure of Truly Lapsed, confirming they represent
defection from an established pattern rather than early-stage churn.

**Why tenure discriminates:** A household with 300 days of tenure and a
widening gap is a different intervention target than one with 150 days of
tenure and no gap — the first is a retention problem, the second is an
acquisition failure. Treating them identically (as a single "lapsed" bucket)
wastes win-back spend on households that were never reliably retained.
